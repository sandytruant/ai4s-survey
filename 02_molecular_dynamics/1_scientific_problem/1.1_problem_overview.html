1.1 Problem Overview: Molecular Dynamics (MD)

Simulating atomic trajectories via Newton's equations: from femtosecond bond vibrations to microsecond biological & materials phenomena.

What Is Molecular Dynamics? (A Computer Scientist's View)

At its core, Molecular Dynamics (MD) is a discrete-time $N$-body particle simulation. Given $N$ atoms with positions $\mathbf{R} = \{\mathbf{r}_1, \dots, \mathbf{r}_N\} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$ and velocities $\mathbf{V} = \{\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_N\}$, MD advances the system step-by-step in time by computing the force on every atom as the negative gradient of a scalar potential energy surface $E(\mathbf{R})$:

$$\mathbf{F}_i(t) = -\nabla_{\mathbf{r}_i} E(\mathbf{R}(t)), \qquad m_i \frac{d^2 \mathbf{r}_i}{dt^2} = \mathbf{F}_i(t)$$

To preserve energy and time-reversibility (symplectic integration), MD uses the Velocity Verlet algorithm with a tiny timestep $\Delta t \approx 1\text{ fs}$ ($10^{-15}\text{ s}$)—dictated by the fastest physical motion (C–H or O–H bond stretching at $\sim 10\text{ fs}$ periods):

$$\mathbf{r}_i(t+\Delta t) = \mathbf{r}_i(t) + \mathbf{v}_i(t)\Delta t + \frac{\mathbf{F}_i(t)}{2m_i}\Delta t^2, \qquad \mathbf{v}_i(t+\Delta t) = \mathbf{v}_i(t) + \frac{\mathbf{F}_i(t) + \mathbf{F}_i(t+\Delta t)}{2m_i}\Delta t$$
Figure 1: The canonical MD time-integration loop. Force evaluation dominates compute, while sequential data dependencies force strict step-by-step execution.

Spatial Locality: Cutoff Radius & Neighbor Lists

Evaluating all $O(N^2)$ pairwise interactions is prohibitive for large $N$. Because short-range chemical bonds and van der Waals forces decay rapidly, MD introduces a spherical cutoff radius $r_c$ (typically $5\text{–}12\text{ \AA}$) around each atom $i$. Only neighbors $j \in \mathcal{N}(i)$ within $r_c$ contribute to short-range forces, reducing complexity from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N \cdot \bar{d})$, where $\bar{d} \approx 20\text{–}100$ is the average neighbor degree.

Figure 2: Cutoff radius $r_c$ induces a dynamic 3D spatial graph stored in Compressed Sparse Row (CSR) or padded Verlet neighbor lists.

Representative Real-World Use Cases

Drug Discovery & Biophysics

Simulating a protein (~$10^4\text{–}10^5$ atoms) solvated in an explicit water box to observe ligand binding, allosteric conformational changes, and protein folding over $1\text{–}100\,\mu\text{s}$ ($10^9\text{–}10^{11}$ steps).

Battery & Solid Electrolytes

Tracking $\text{Li}^+$ ion hopping and diffusion coefficients in solid-state electrolytes (e.g., $\text{Li}_{10}\text{GeP}_2\text{S}_{12}$) or SEI formation at electrode interfaces, requiring reactive bond-breaking accuracy.

Materials Fracture & Phase Transitions

Modeling crack propagation, dislocation glide, or crystal nucleation across $10^6\text{–}10^8$ atoms where mesoscale defects interact with atomistic bond breaking.
