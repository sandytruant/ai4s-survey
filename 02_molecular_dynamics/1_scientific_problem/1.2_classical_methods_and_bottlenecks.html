1.2 Classical MD vs. Ab Initio MD & Architectural Bottlenecks

The fundamental $O(N)$ vs. $O(N^3)$ accuracy-speed chasm, irregular neighbor gathers, and 3D FFT all-to-all communication walls.

Two Classical Paradigms for Computing $\mathbf{F}_i = -\nabla_i E(\mathbf{R})$

Classical Empirical MD

Fixed-Topology Force Fields (AMBER, CHARMM, OPLS)

Approximates $E(\mathbf{R})$ with analytical pairwise/bonded terms: harmonic springs (bonds/angles), dihedrals, short-range Lennard-Jones ($r^{-12} - r^{-6}$), and long-range Coulomb electrostatics via Particle-Mesh Ewald (PME).

  • Complexity: $O(N)$ short-range + $O(N \log N)$ PME FFT.
  • Speed: Millions of atom-steps/sec on a single GPU.
  • Limitation: Fixed bond graph—cannot model chemical reactions, charge transfer, or polarization accurately.
Ab Initio MD (AIMD)

Quantum-Mechanical Forces (DFT / Car-Parrinello / Born-Oppenheimer)

Solves the Kohn-Sham Density Functional Theory (DFT) electronic structure equations at every single MD timestep to obtain exact Hellmann-Feynman forces $\mathbf{F}_i$.

  • Complexity: $O(N^3)$ per step (dense matrix diagonalization / orthogonalization).
  • Speed: $\sim 100\text{–}1{,}000$ atoms for $\sim 10\text{–}100\text{ ps}$ on supercomputers.
  • Limitation: Computationally intractable for $N > 10^4$ or $\mu\text{s}$ timescales.

Hardware & Compiler Bottlenecks in Classical MD

Bottleneck Computational Root Cause Hardware / Compiler Impact
Irregular Memory Access Neighbor list traversal gathers coordinates $\mathbf{r}_j$ from non-contiguous indices $j \in \mathcal{N}(i)$ and scatters forces $\mathbf{F}_j$ back (Newton's 3rd law). Low cache-line utilization, uncoalesced GPU global memory loads, atomic-add contention on force accumulation, poor SIMD vectorization without spatial sorting (e.g., Hilbert/Z-order curves).
3D FFT All-to-All (PME) Particle-Mesh Ewald interpolates charges onto a 3D grid, runs forward/inverse 3D FFTs, and gathers reciprocal forces. Requires global pencil/slab transposes (MPI_Alltoall). At high node counts (strong scaling), latency of small messages dominates—capping single-protein scaling around $100\text{–}1{,}000$ GPUs/nodes.
Load Imbalance Spatial domain decomposition assigns fixed bounding boxes to MPI ranks, but atom density varies (e.g., vacuum gaps, lipid membranes, crack tips). Ranks with denser sub-domains become stragglers at every step's halo exchange barrier; requires dynamic load balancing (e.g., recursive coordinate bisection).
Low Arithmetic Intensity & Launch Overhead Empirical LJ + Coulomb evaluation requires only $\sim 20\text{–}50$ FLOPs per pair; step times can be $< 100\,\mu\text{s}$ on GPUs. CPU-GPU kernel launch latency, PCIe/NVLink host-device sync, and bond/angle/constraint kernels become Amdahl bottlenecks when non-bonded force kernels are fast.
