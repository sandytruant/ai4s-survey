1.3 Compute Demand & Scientific Impact

Why MD hits a brutal strong-scaling latency wall: $10^6\text{–}10^9$ sequential steps, microsecond-per-step budgets, and the cost of insufficient speed.

The Tyranny of the Femtosecond Timestep

Unlike embarrassingly parallel workloads or large-batch AI training, a single MD trajectory is strictly sequential in time. You cannot compute $\mathbf{R}(t + \Delta t)$ until $\mathbf{F}(t)$ is known. Reaching biologically or technologically relevant timescales requires chaining millions to billions of steps back-to-back:

System Scales & Strong-Scaling vs. Weak-Scaling

Simulation Regime Atom Count ($N$) Target Timescale Primary Scaling Challenge
Biomolecular Drug Binding $3 \times 10^4 \text{ – } 2 \times 10^5$ $1\text{ – }100\,\mu\text{s}$ ($10^9\text{–}10^{11}$ steps) Strong scaling: Small $N$ spread across many chips; step latency is bounded by MPI/interconnect latency & 3D FFTs.
Solid-State Electrolytes / Catalysis $10^3 \text{ – } 5 \times 10^4$ $1\text{ – }100\text{ ns}$ ($10^6\text{–}10^8$ steps) Quantum accuracy + speed: Requires reactive/electronic-structure fidelity at $10^4\times\text{–}10^6\times$ DFT throughput.
Materials Fracture / Shock / Virus Capsid $10^6 \text{ – } 10^8+$ $1\text{ – }100\text{ ns}$ ($10^6\text{–}10^8$ steps) Weak scaling & memory: Ghost-atom halo exchanges, GPU HBM capacity, and spatial load imbalance.

What Happens When Compute Performance Is Insufficient?

1. Missed Rare Events

Chemical reactions, crystal nucleation, and drug unbinding are thermally activated rare events. A simulation stuck at $10\text{ ns}$ simply never observes transitions that occur on $\mu\text{s}$ timescales.

2. Finite-Size Artifacts

Shrinking the simulation box ($N < 1{,}000$ atoms) to afford AIMD introduces artificial periodic image interactions, suppressing long-wavelength phonons, defect loops, and realistic solvation shells.

3. Screening Bottleneck

Evaluating thousands of candidate battery electrolytes, alloys, or drug-target complexes is impossible if each trajectory consumes weeks of supercomputer allocation.
