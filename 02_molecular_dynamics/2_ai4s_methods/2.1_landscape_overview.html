2.1 AI4S Landscape Overview: Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials

Replacing $O(N^3)$ quantum DFT force evaluations with $O(N)$ neural networks—and understanding the non-AI kernels that remain in the MD loop.

What AI Replaces vs. What Non-AI Computation Remains

In mainstream AI-accelerated MD, neural networks—called Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials (MLIPs) or Machine Learning Force Fields (MLFFs)—act as surrogate models trained on DFT energies and forces. They decompose total energy into per-atom contributions $E(\mathbf{R}) = \sum_{i=1}^N E_i(\mathcal{N}(i))$ and compute forces via reverse-mode automatic differentiation $\mathbf{F}_i = -\nabla_{\mathbf{r}_i} E$.

From an Amdahl's Law perspective, AI does not replace the MD engine (such as LAMMPS or ASE); it replaces only the potential energy and force kernel inside each Velocity Verlet timestep.

Representative Methods (Deep-Dive Pages)

DeePMD-kit

Descriptor-based deep potential achieving 100M+ atoms on Summit and 149 ns/day on Fugaku via custom fused kernels and mixed precision.

NequIP & Allegro

$E(3)$-equivariant GNNs vs. strictly local tensor-product architectures that eliminate multi-hop halo blowup to scale to 100M atoms on A100 clusters.

MACE

Many-body equivariant MPNNs combining Atomic Cluster Expansion with cuEquivariance CUDA primitives for 3–5× inference speedups.

SevenNet (+ FlashTP)

Multi-GPU spatial-decomposition equivariant GNN in LAMMPS (>80% weak scaling) paired with sparsity-aware fused FlashTP kernels.
