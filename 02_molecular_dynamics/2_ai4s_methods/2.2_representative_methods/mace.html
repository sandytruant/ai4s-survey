MACE: Higher-Body-Order Equivariant Message Passing

Combining Atomic Cluster Expansion (ACE) symmetric contractions with 2-layer $E(3)$-equivariant MPNNs, accelerated 3–5× by NVIDIA cuEquivariance.

Many-Body Equivariant MPNN cuEquivariance / A100 / H100  |  GitHub: ACEsuit/mace  |  NeurIPS 2022 Paper (arXiv:2206.07697)

2.2.1 Overall Workflow: AI vs. Non-AI Pipeline

Standard MPNNs pass only 2-body messages per layer, requiring 4–6 layers to capture many-body geometry. MACE pools 2-body edge messages onto each node $i$ and applies a symmetric tensor contraction (from ACE, up to body-order $\nu = 3$ or $4$) inside each layer. This achieves high accuracy with just 2 message-passing layers, shrinking the receptive field from $5\cdot r_c$ to $2\cdot r_c$.

Foundation Model Role: MACE-MP-0 and MACE-OFF23 serve as universal pretrained potentials across the periodic table and organic molecules.

2.2.3 Dedicated Accelerators & Kernel Libraries

  • NVIDIA cuEquivariance (NVIDIA/cuEquivariance): Dedicated CUDA library of Segmented Tensor Products and symmetric contraction primitives natively integrated into MACE (≥ v0.3.11).
  • OpenEquivariance & FlashTP: Open-source portable CUDA/HIP/PyTorch kernels targeting Clebsch-Gordan tensor products and symmetric contractions across NVIDIA and AMD GPUs.
