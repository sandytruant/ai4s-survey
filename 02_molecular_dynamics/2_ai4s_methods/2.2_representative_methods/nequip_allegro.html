NequIP & Allegro: Equivariant GNNs vs. Strictly Local Tensor Products

From $E(3)$-equivariant message passing (NequIP) to strictly local edge-based tensor products (Allegro) that eliminate multi-hop halo blowup to reach 100M atoms.

E(3)-Equivariant LAMMPS Kokkos / A100  |  GitHub: mir-group/nequip  |  GitHub: mir-group/allegro  |  NequIP (Nat. Commun. 2022)  |  Allegro (Nat. Commun. 2023)

2.2.1 Overall Workflow: Why Allegro Drops Message Passing

NequIP introduced $E(3)$-equivariant message passing using irreducible representations (irreps) of $O(3)$ and Clebsch-Gordan tensor products, achieving unprecedented data efficiency. However, $L$ message-passing layers expand the effective receptive field to $L \cdot r_c$ (e.g., $4 \times 5\text{ \AA} = 20\text{ \AA}$), causing cubic growth in MPI ghost-atom halo volume and blocking multi-GPU scaling.

Allegro solves this architectural bottleneck by removing atom-centered message passing entirely. It maintains strict locality within a single cutoff $r_c$ by iterating equivariant tensor products directly on directed edge features $(i, j)$ combined with a scalar latent track.

Compiler Insight: Replacing multi-hop MPNN with strictly local edge tensor products cuts halo communication from O((L·r_c)³) to O(r_c³).

2.2.2 Computational Profiling & Bottlenecks

  • Extreme Scale Milestone: Integrated with LAMMPS via Kokkos/LibTorch, Allegro demonstrated production MD simulations exceeding 100 million atoms on NVIDIA A100 GPU clusters (e.g., NERSC Perlmutter), maintaining near-linear weak scaling because each GPU only exchanges a single-$r_c$ ghost halo.
  • Compute & Memory Bottlenecks: Standard implementations rely on e3nn / PyTorch JIT, where Clebsch-Gordan tensor products on every directed edge $(i, j)$ generate many small, memory-bound kernels, high register pressure, and large intermediate activation tensors saved for autograd backward force computation ($\mathbf{F}_i = -\nabla_i E$).

2.2.3 Dedicated Accelerators & Kernel Libraries

  • NVIDIA cuEquivariance & OpenEquivariance / FlashTP: Specialized CUDA tensor-product libraries fuse sparse Clebsch-Gordan contractions into single GPU kernels, eliminating intermediate DRAM round-trips.
  • LAMMPS-Kokkos / TorchScript / AOTInductor: Compiles the PyTorch graph ahead of time to remove Python GIL and dispatch latency during tight microsecond-scale MD loops.
