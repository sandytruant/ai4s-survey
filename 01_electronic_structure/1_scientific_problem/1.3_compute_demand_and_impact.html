1.3 Compute Demand & Scientific Impact

Why electronic structure codes consume a massive fraction of global supercomputer cycles—and what physics is lost when compute is insufficient.

Supercomputer Cycle Consumption

Electronic structure simulations are among the single largest consumers of leadership-class HPC allocations worldwide. Unlike AI training workloads that saturate Tensor Cores in BF16/FP8, classical DFT codes demand FP64 throughput, high memory bandwidth, and low-latency interconnects.

NERSC (DOE Flagship)
Materials science and chemistry account for ~20–30%+ of total node-hours. Historically, the plane-wave DFT code VASP alone has consumed ~20% of NERSC's entire supercomputer workload, alongside Quantum ESPRESSO, NWChem, and CP2K.
Fugaku (RIKEN, A64FX)
Plane-wave DFT codes (Quantum ESPRESSO, VASP) and real-space DFT codes (RSDFT) form a core tier of Fugaku's materials discovery and industrial allocations, heavily exercising its 1 TB/s HBM2 bandwidth per node.
Frontier (ORNL Exascale)
GPU-ported electronic structure codes (VASP/OpenACC, Quantum ESPRESSO GPU, LSMS) run large-scale INCITE/ALCC materials campaigns, yet 3D FFT all-to-all communication and $O(N^3)$ eigensolvers limit strong scaling across thousands of GPUs.
