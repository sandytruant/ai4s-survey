Paradigm (b): Efficient SE(3)-Equivariant Molecular Hamiltonians

QHNet

Cutting Clebsch-Gordan tensor products by 92% and GPU memory by 50% for SE(3)-equivariant molecular Hamiltonian prediction across MD17 and QH9.

Verified Sources: Paper (ICLR 2024 / arXiv:2306.04922) | GitHub: divelab/AIRS

2.2.1 Overall Workflow: AI vs. Non-AI Kernels

Earlier molecular Hamiltonian networks (e.g., PhiSNet) suffered from massive tensor product (TP) overhead and exponential channel growth with diverse atom types. QHNet uses a streamlined SE(3)-equivariant node/pair interaction architecture to predict $H(\mathbf{R})$ in one forward pass, followed by a single classical generalized eigensolve $HC = SCE$.

2.2.2 Computational Profiling & Bottlenecks

Metric / Aspect Observed Behavior & Published Profiling (ICLR 2024)
Tensor Product Reduction Reduces the number of Clebsch-Gordan tensor product (TP) operations by 92% compared to baseline PhiSNet.
Runtime & Speedup Achieves 3.6× to 3.8× faster runtime (FP64 benchmarks on MD17 systems such as Water and Ethanol) compared to PhiSNet, while maintaining competitive MAE on MD17 and the large-scale QH9 benchmark.
Memory Footprint Consumes 50% less GPU memory than PhiSNet by avoiding redundant intermediate irreps expansions and atom-type channel blowup.
Precision & Amdahl's Law Benchmarked in FP64 for Hamiltonian accuracy. As AI inference becomes 3.8× faster, the 1-shot generalized eigenvalue solve $HC=SCE$ ($O(N^3)$ in orbital count) becomes the dominant Amdahl limit for larger molecules.

2.2.3 Dedicated Accelerators & Kernel Libraries

  • Clebsch-Gordan Kernel Accelerators: Just like DeepH-E3, QHNet's remaining tensor product and expansion blocks map directly onto NVIDIA cuEquivariance, OpenEquivariance, and FlashTP fused GPU kernels.
  • Tensor Core Utilization Challenge: Small irreps ($l=0,1,2,3,4$) produce irregular, small-matrix contractions that underutilize standard $16\times 16$ Tensor Cores unless batched and fused at the compiler level.
