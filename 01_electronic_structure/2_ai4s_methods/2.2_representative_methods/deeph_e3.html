Paradigm (b): Equivariant DFT Hamiltonian Prediction

DeepH-E3

Bypassing the iterative DFT SCF loop for $10^4$+ atom supercells via $E(3)$-equivariant graph neural networks, leaving a single sparse/generalized eigensolve.

Verified Sources: Paper (Nature Communications 2023 / arXiv:2210.13955) | GitHub: Xiaoxun-Gong/DeepH-E3

2.2.1 Overall Workflow: AI vs. Non-AI Kernels

In localized-orbital DFT (OpenMX, ABACUS, SIESTA, FHI-aims), the Hamiltonian $H_{i\alpha, j\beta}$ is a sparse, block-structured matrix whose entries depend only on local atomic environments ("nearsightedness of electronic matter") and transform equivariantly under 3D rotations $R \in E(3)$.

Figure 2.3: DeepH-E3 pipeline. An $E(3)$-equivariant GNN predicts the sparse Hamiltonian blocks $H_{ij}$ in $O(N)$ time, bypassing all SCF iterations.

2.2.2 Computational Profiling & Bottlenecks

Metric / Aspect Observed Behavior & Published Profiling
Scale & Speedup Trained on small DFT unit cells, DeepH-E3 predicts Hamiltonians for supercells exceeding $10^4$ atoms (e.g., magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene) at sub-meV accuracy, achieving 2–3+ orders of magnitude speedup over direct SCF DFT.
AI Kernel Bottleneck (e3nn) The PyTorch/e3nn Clebsch-Gordan (CG) tensor product layers dominate GPU inference time. Standard e3nn executes un-fused, memory-bound kernels with low Tensor Core utilization and high intermediate tensor traffic.
Non-AI Bottleneck ($HC = SCE$) Once $H$ is predicted in seconds, solving $HC = SCE$ for all eigenstates still scales as $O(N^3)$. DeepH-E3 mitigates this using sparse iterative solvers (Julia Arpack.jl, IterativeSolvers.jl) when only bands near the Fermi level are needed.
Precision & Data Layout GNN inference runs in FP32, but the final predicted Hamiltonian blocks must be assembled in FP64 for numerical stability during sparse/dense eigensolving.

2.2.3 Dedicated Accelerators & Kernel Libraries

  • NVIDIA cuEquivariance: Drop-in CUDA-X library for Segmented Tensor Products, delivering up to 10× end-to-end speedups and up to 200× on symmetric contractions compared to e3nn by fusing operations in GPU shared memory.
  • OpenEquivariance (PASSIONLab): Open-source JIT sparse kernel generator for $O(3)$-equivariant CG tensor products across CUDA, HIP, and SYCL, achieving >10× speedup over e3nn and 1.3×–4.8× over cuEquivariance on CG kernels.
  • FlashTP (ICML): Sparsity-aware, path-aggregated fused GPU kernels for equivariant tensor products, achieving up to 41.6× kernel speedup over e3nn and ~6.3× peak memory reduction.
  • Distributed Eigensolvers (ELPA / PEXSI / cuSOLVER): For dense or pole-expansion solves of $HC = SCE$, GPU-accelerated eigensolvers remain essential to prevent Amdahl's Law saturation.
