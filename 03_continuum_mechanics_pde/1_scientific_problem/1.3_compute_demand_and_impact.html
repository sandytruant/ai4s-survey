1.3 Compute Demand & Real-World Impact

Why 3D resolution, turbulence $Re^{9/4}$ scaling, and parametric design sweeps exhaust supercomputers—and what breaks when compute is insufficient.

The $O(N^4)$ Curse of Spatio-Temporal Resolution

Refining a 3D mesh by a factor of $2\times$ in each spatial dimension increases cell count by $2^3 = 8\times$. Because explicit/semi-implicit time steps must shrink proportionally ($\Delta t \propto \Delta x$) to satisfy the CFL stability condition, total work scales as $O(N^4)$ (or $O(Re^3)$ in Direct Numerical Simulation of turbulence).

Figure 1: Compute demand compounds across spatial resolution ($N^3$), CFL time steps ($N_t$), and outer-loop parametric sweeps.

Consequences of Insufficient Compute Performance

Missed Boundary Layers & Turbulence
Coarsening the mesh to fit memory/time budgets smears out thin viscous boundary layers, shockwaves, and vortex shedding—corrupting drag, lift, and thermal hotspot predictions.
Slow Engineering Design Cycles
When a single high-fidelity CFD or EM signoff run takes 12–48 hours on a cluster, engineers can only test a handful of CAD variants, bottlenecking aircraft, EV, and 3D-IC tapeouts.
No Real-Time Digital Twins
Active flow control, tokamak plasma disruption avoidance, and rapid 50-member weather ensembles require sub-second to second-scale inference that classical solvers cannot meet.
