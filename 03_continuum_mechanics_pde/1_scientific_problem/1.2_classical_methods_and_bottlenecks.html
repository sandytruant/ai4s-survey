1.2 Classical PDE Solvers & Architectural Bottlenecks

Why FDM, FVM, FEM, and Spectral solvers hit the memory wall, synchronization wall, and CFL time-step barrier.

Four Classical Discretization Paradigms

Method Discretization Core Compute Kernel Primary Hardware Bottleneck
Finite Difference (FDM) Structured Cartesian grid 7-pt / 27-pt dense stencil loops DRAM bandwidth (arithmetic intensity $\approx 0.1\text{–}0.5\text{ FLOP/B}$)
Finite Volume (FVM) Unstructured polyhedral cells (e.g., OpenFOAM) Face-flux loops + CSR SpMV (A*x) Indirect gather-scatter memory access, low cache reuse
Finite Element (FEM) Unstructured elements + basis functions Element-matrix assembly + global sparse solve Irregular scatter-add during assembly; ill-conditioned SpMV
Spectral Methods Global Fourier / Chebyshev basis 3D FFT / iFFT + pointwise mode ops All-to-all transpose communication across nodes/GPUs

Architectural & Compiler Bottlenecks

Classical PDE solvers are among the lowest arithmetic-intensity workloads in HPC. On modern GPUs (e.g., NVIDIA H100 with ~67 TFLOP/s FP64 and ~3.35 TB/s HBM3, ridge point $\approx 20\text{ FLOP/B}$), a sparse matrix-vector multiply (SpMV) or 7-point stencil achieves less than 5% of peak compute.

Figure 1: Left: Classical SpMV and stencil kernels sit far left on the memory-bandwidth slope of the Roofline model. Right: Each time step requires hundreds of Krylov/Multigrid iterations punctuated by global Allreduce barriers.

1. Irregular Memory Access & Compiler Limits
On unstructured FVM/FEM meshes, neighbor lists stored in CSR/COO format cause non-coalesced DRAM reads (val[col_idx[j]]). Compilers cannot statically vectorize or prefetch indirect gathers without inspector-executor reordering (e.g., Cuthill-McKee), leaving caches underutilized.
2. Global Synchronization & CFL Barrier
Krylov solvers (CG, GMRES) require inner products every iteration, triggering latency-bound MPI_Allreduce barriers across nodes. Meanwhile, the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) condition ($\text{CFL} = |\mathbf{u}|\Delta t / \Delta x \le 1$) forces tiny $\Delta t$ whenever meshes are refined.
Precision Sensitivity: Iterative linear solvers and long-horizon time integration typically require FP64 (double precision) to avoid catastrophic cancellation and preserve conservation laws—preventing direct use of low-precision FP16/FP8 Tensor Cores without mixed-precision iterative refinement.
