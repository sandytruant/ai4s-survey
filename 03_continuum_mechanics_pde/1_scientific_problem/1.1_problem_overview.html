1.1 Macroscopic Continuum Mechanics PDEs

Translating continuous physical fields—fluids, solids, heat, and electromagnetics—into discrete grids, graphs, and tensor workloads.

What Is Continuum Mechanics?

Instead of tracking $10^{23}$ individual atoms (Pillar 2), continuum mechanics models matter as a smooth, continuous medium. Every point $\mathbf{x} \in \Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ at time $t$ carries macroscopic field values—velocity $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, t)$, pressure $p(\mathbf{x}, t)$, stress $\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{x}, t)$, temperature $T(\mathbf{x}, t)$, or electromagnetic fields $(\mathbf{E}, \mathbf{B})$.

Their evolution is governed by Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) expressing local conservation laws (mass, momentum, energy, charge): what flows into a tiny control volume minus what flows out equals the rate of accumulation plus internal sources.

Core Governing Equations

From a computer architect's viewpoint, most continuum simulations solve one of four canonical PDE families, each imposing distinct coupling, stiffness, and precision demands:

1. Fluid Dynamics: Navier-Stokes

$$\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{f}, \quad \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$$

Governs incompressible fluids ($\rho$ density, $\nu$ kinematic viscosity). Nonlinear advection $(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}$ creates multi-scale turbulence, while the divergence-free constraint $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0$ forces a global elliptic Poisson solve for pressure $p$ at every time step.

2. Solid Mechanics: Elasticity

$$\rho \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{d}}{\partial t^2} = \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{d}) + \mathbf{f}, \quad \boldsymbol{\sigma} = \mathbf{C} : \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{d})$$

Governs structural displacement $\mathbf{d}$ and stress tensor $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ (linear Hooke's law or nonlinear hyperelasticity/plasticity). Typically discretized via Finite Elements (FEM), yielding large symmetric positive-definite (or ill-conditioned) sparse linear systems $K\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{f}$.

3. Thermal Transport: Heat / Diffusion

$$\rho c_p \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot (k \nabla T) + q_{\text{src}}$$

Parabolic PDE governing temperature $T$ with thermal conductivity $k$ and heat source $q_{\text{src}}$ (e.g., power density in a 3D-IC). Explicit stepping is severely constrained by $\Delta t \propto \Delta x^2$, making implicit linear solves standard.

4. Electromagnetics: Maxwell's Equations

$$\frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\nabla \times \mathbf{H} - \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbf{J}, \quad \frac{\partial \mathbf{H}}{\partial t} = -\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla \times \mathbf{E}$$

Hyperbolic wave propagation of electric ($\mathbf{E}$) and magnetic ($\mathbf{H}$) fields. Often solved via staggered Yee-grid Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) stencils—highly regular, memory-bandwidth bound, and sensitive to phase dispersion.
