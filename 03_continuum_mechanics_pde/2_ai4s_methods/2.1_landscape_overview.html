2.1 AI4S Landscape for Continuum PDEs

Four architectural families replacing iterative PDE solvers—and the non-AI preprocessing, meshing, and hybrid correction kernels that remain.

Four AI4S Paradigms & Their Hardware Signatures

1. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)

PINNs & NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo

Represent $u_\theta(\mathbf{x}, t)$ as an MLP/coordinate network trained with PDE residuals via automatic differentiation. Compute profile: Higher-order autograd (create_graph=True) creates deep backward graphs and small-batch memory/kernel-launch bottlenecks.

2. Neural Operators

Fourier Neural Operator (FNO)

Learn mappings between function spaces by parameterizing linear integral kernels in Fourier space via FFT/iFFT. Compute profile: Dense 2D/3D FFT transposes (memory-BW/all-to-all bound) paired with batched complex GEMMs on truncated modes.

3. Graph-Based Simulators

MeshGraphNets (GNN)

Encode unstructured FEM/FVM meshes as graphs and run $L$ steps of learned message passing. Compute profile: Irregular scatter_add / gather across variable-degree edges—memory-bandwidth bound with poor spatial locality.

4. PDE Foundation Models

Transolver & Poseidon

Physics-Attention (Transolver) projects $N$ mesh points into $M \ll N$ physical slices for $O(N)$ linear attention; multiscale operator transformers (Poseidon) pretrain across PDE families. Compute profile: Tensor-Core-friendly dense GEMMs.

Amdahl's Law View: What AI Replaces vs. What Remains Non-AI

Even when AI achieves a $100\times$–$1000\times$ speedup on the core PDE time-stepping or Poisson solve, end-to-end engineering pipelines still rely on classical non-AI kernels for geometry, boundary enforcement, and physical conservation.
