PINNs & NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo

Mesh-free continuous PDE surrogates trained via higher-order automatic differentiation of governing equations.

Coordinate MLP / Autograd PDE Raissi et al. (arXiv:1711.10561) • JCP 2019 DOI • GitHub: NVIDIA/physicsnemo • PhysicsNeMo Docs

2.2.1 Overall Workflow

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) parameterize the solution field $u_\theta(\mathbf{x}, t)$ as a neural network mapping continuous coordinates $(\mathbf{x}, t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ to physical quantities $(\mathbf{u}, p, T)$. Instead of generating a volumetric mesh, NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo (physicsnemo.sym, formerly Modulus) samples collocation points inside the CAD geometry and minimizes a composite loss of boundary conditions and symbolic PDE residuals.

Figure 1: PINN training pipeline in PhysicsNeMo. Computing second-order PDE residuals ($\nabla^2 u$) requires retaining the computation graph (create_graph=True), turning optimizer backprop into a 3rd-order differentiation sweep.

2.2.2 Computational Profiling & Bottlenecks

Metric / Aspect Observed Characteristics (Raissi et al. / PhysicsNeMo Docs)
Hardware & Scaling PyTorch + CUDA GPUs; PhysicsNeMo supports multi-GPU / multi-node scaling via ShardTensor domain parallelism and torch.compile.
Inference vs. Training Once trained, forward evaluation is a single MLP pass (milliseconds on GPU), avoiding iterative linear solves. However, training is slow and must be re-run when geometry/BCs change unless parameterized.
Primary Bottleneck: Higher-Order Autograd Computing $\nabla^2 u$ for Navier-Stokes or elasticity requires nested reverse-mode AD (create_graph=True). The intermediate activation tape grows rapidly, capping collocation batch size in GPU HBM and incurring heavy Python/kernel-launch overhead.
Precision & Conditioning Smooth activations (SiLU, tanh) and PDE residual sums often require FP32 (or TF32/FP64 for stiff boundary layers); multi-scale loss terms compete during gradient descent.
Compiler Insight: Standard eager PyTorch autograd launches dozens of tiny elementwise kernels for second-order derivatives. Fusing the forward MLP and symbolic derivative graph via torch.compile, JAX (jax.grad + jax.vmap), or Separable PINNs (SPINNs) dramatically reduces HBM traffic and launch latency.

2.2.3 Dedicated Accelerators & Compiler Stacks

  • NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo + torch.compile + Nsight Systems: Built-in kernel fusion, GPU-optimized layers, and Nsight profiling harnesses for tracing autograd hotspots.
  • JAX / XLA (jax.jvp / vjp + vmap): Ahead-of-time XLA whole-graph compilation fuses higher-order Taylor-mode / forward-over-reverse AD into single GPU kernels, eliminating Python tape overhead.
  • NVIDIA Warp (warp.fem): Python-to-CUDA JIT compiler for hybrid physics-ML where exact FEM/stencil derivatives replace expensive AD tapes.
  • Tensor Cores (TF32 / BF16) vs. FP32: MLP layers map well to Tensor Cores, though higher-order derivative accumulation typically stays in FP32 to avoid gradient underflow.
